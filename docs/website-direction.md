# Current creative direction

Updated 27 September 2026. This is the current design brief. [The manifesto](../MANIFESTO.md) gives the enduring purpose; this document resolves choices made since it was written. Earlier mock/storyboard notes remain history, not competing instructions.

## The experience

A personal world, strong work and a quiet invitation to collaborate. The homepage tells a scenic story about Glen; Work and Writing are separate immersive rooms. Senior engineering experience, initiative and problem solving should be evident in how the website works and in useful things visitors can try. Avoid a homepage full of project cards or explanations of each picture.

The [shared art style](art-style.md) is the single visual definition for every asset across scenes, pages and article covers. It owns the approved references, rendering treatment, character identity, asset review and future style changes. Runtime art and derived motion assets live in `public/assets/world/`.

## Characters and copy

Follow the [character and motion rules](art-style.md#the-visual-language) for Glen's identity, anatomy and complete poses. Scene-specific contact and choreography remain with each scene.

The voice is direct, cheeky and personal: “Skipping leg day since 2008.” Read the original posts, especially “Lottery of Birth”, “Bone to Be Wild”, “The Russian Connection” and “Do you have an ideal dream job?”, before writing copy. The draft is editable. Avoid generic portfolio slogans, narrated pictures, exaggerated business claims and explanations of the technology in visitor-facing UI.

## Homepage copy and discoveries

The three scenes read as one introduction through the day, without a repeated eyebrow/headline/tagline formula. The lake starts with “I’m Glen, a software engineer.”, whose second line retypes itself once through lighthearted, true alternatives (“a maker of things”, “a human in the loop”, “a fixer of workflows”, “an AI meat proxy”) and settles back; the heading's text and accessible name stay the authored line, and it is static when motion is paused or reduced. The leg-day line is now the pull-up bar marker's caption and opens “The Lottery of Birth”. The beach carries Glen's point of view: an engineer at Remote.com who builds for problems worth solving, with AI where it helps and without it where it doesn't. The city turns to life and stories, then a curious, low-pressure invitation to swap notes. It deliberately does not offer services: colleagues at Remote.com read this site too.

Keep paragraphs naturally wrapping and the later scene headings quieter than the opening. Copy is scene-owned and editable; do not narrate the picture. Let the website demonstrate its creativity and engineering. The beach’s “About this little world” popup and self-explanatory website aside were removed at Glen’s request. Do not add visitor-facing explanations that the website was built with AI, or narrate ordinary technical details such as HTML links and motion controls.

## Typography

Glen selected **Lora for headings and the italic signature**, with **Nunito Sans for body text and navigation**, on 27 September 2026. Apply this pairing consistently across the scenic homepage, rooms and dialogs. Keep wording and line breaks editable, and check portrait wrapping after copy or layout changes.

The fonts are self-hosted under `public/assets/fonts/diorama/`, with source records and licenses alongside them. Shared declarations and font tokens live in `features/diorama/styles/typography.css`; scene styles should inherit those tokens rather than introduce competing font families.

## Movement and discovery

The homepage follows one day: **dawn at the lake → midday at the beach → night in the city**. A single persistent sky changes with native scroll while the ground, characters and scene objects transition beneath it. Keep cloud positions continuous, let the sun rise and set, and bring in the moon and stars as daylight fades. The original landscape art blends into that shared sky; avoid three separate sky panels or long empty transition bands. Portrait has its own celestial placement so the copy stays readable.

The sun starts peeking over the lake's right-hand ridge and follows one east-to-west (right-to-left) arc. Morning scroll starts its ascent immediately and gently brightens the lake. Sunset and moonrise overlap: the sun lowers on the left as the moon climbs from the right, with faint stars gradually emerging. These movements reverse naturally when scrolling back.

Later character routines may follow that day (for example, alternating pull-ups and laptop work at the lake). Those routines are future work; the current change is the sky and its environmental motion.

The initial v1 felt too static. The target is visible character actions plus restrained environmental movement, not simply parallax applied to a flat picture. Native reversible scrolling connects scenes; eliminate long muddy gradient bands and forced pauses. Keep the terrain, characters, props and their discovery markers grounded in one fixed composition during scroll. Hand over between scenes with a shaped, scroll-driven wipe while the shared sky advances through the day: the beach's tide rolls in from the horizon over the lake, and dusk sweeps the city in from the right, following the sun west. Never blend two paintings into a muddy double exposure. The outgoing character leaves just ahead of the edge and the incoming one settles in after it passes, so Glen never appears twice; movement within a scene comes from character actions and environmental animation, not separate vertical scroll offsets. A small down arrow is enough of a scroll prompt. After the city, a short epilogue lets the city's words give way to “That’s the day.” with Work, Writing, contact and social links and a way to start the day again (copy in `data/ending.ts`, editable). Navigation should feel part of the scene without enclosing header/footer panels.

Use a **small outlined four-point glint** anchored to interactive objects. This replaces the floating glowing dot, which could be mistaken for the sun or part of the scenery. One shared component sets appearance, target size, focus and label behavior. Labels appear on hover/focus instead of being permanently displayed. Real links/buttons and ordinary navigation remain available; discovery is a pleasure, not a prerequisite. Recognisable objects can replace the glint: the beach GitHub sticker and beach phone use the same shared accessible link, touch target and hover/focus label. The phone displays schematic feed rows, not invented tweets or a live embed. Light switches, paper shuffling and screen changes should offer deliberate little interactions.

Three.js experiments are welcome wherever they produce a better result. Preserve the illustrated aesthetic: the first lake shader was judged too realistic/fluid and was tuned towards the painting's texture and a gentler rhythm. Compare effects in motion, then retain only those that belong. Text/navigation never depend on a canvas. Honor pause/reduced motion, stop offscreen work, cap resolution, handle unavailable WebGL, and dispose resources.

## Longer-term direction

Articles use a quiet paper reading layout with a dedicated illustrated cover slot, preserving original writing and in-body photographs. Cover art follows [the shared art style](art-style.md#article-covers-and-source-material). Writing links to the updated article previews and shares each article's bespoke cover through one registry. Content still comes from Prismic; comments, likes, repo migration and bespoke interactive essays are separate later work. See [article ownership and workflow](articles.md).

AI conversation with a character, optional voice, useful niche tools and interactive essays can show capability through use. These remain future work unless implemented and verified explicitly. StayPal is an exploration, not a proven product claim. Work history, client outcomes and business results require evidence. Glen's role at Remote.com and 10+ years of experience can appear through scene objects and progressive disclosure.

Fast loading, meaningful SEO, responsive portrait composition, accessible navigation and maintainable code are part of the design. A static/reduced-motion version must still be complete and attractive.
